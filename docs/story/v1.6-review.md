# Story review before v1.6 (2026-11-04)

By the v1.6 storyteller, for Guillaume. Read-through of all story quests and their English text in `src/i18n/en.json` on `main` (prologue, hub, 4 ring arcs, epilogue, Ness's chart, 4 letters, 4 sea arcs, 4 outposts, Four Winds, club hint, regatta and standing-order samples). Counts come from `src/data/quests/`. French was checked only for key parity (461 of 461 quest keys present) and one spot read, not line by line. Builds on the [v1.2 quest review](../reviews/whole-game-v1.2/quests.md); where today's data differs from it, this page says so.

## Verdict

**It makes sense, and it is warm. It is not yet gripping.** The spine is clear and never dead-ends. The voices are the real strength. But after the ring, the story stops surprising: four seas run one template, nearly every step is "fetch X, bring it", and nobody in the story ever wants anything that costs them something. Right for a cozy game, thin for a long game.

## Arc map

```

   v

   v

   v

```

## What lands

- **A cheerful hook.** A foghorn in the dark, a wreck, a harbourmaster who "went looking for the shoals and found the reef". Rook's debt becomes a free island. Clear goal at once.
- **The cast.** 15 people with a speech habit each (Rook's ledger, Maren's "and", Ness wandering off, Sigrun counting winters). Lines are quotable without names. Siblings and cousins across seas (Ines/Oskar, Kit/Nell, Maren/Pip, Ness/Tam) make the map feel connected. Nell's "Kit wrote, did he? First letter in six years" is the best single line in the sea arcs.
- **Ring arcs.** Different sailing in each: a buoyed reef gap, a mast from a harbourless ridge, a timed rush, carrying Rook himself, a lit tower. Each pays off visibly.
- **Set pieces in two seas.** The west ends on an eruption and a new island; the north on a beacon that opens the pack ice. These are the only moments in the seas that feel like events.
- **Tone discipline.** Nothing can fail, no villain, no deadline, kid-safe, and the card rules (2 to 4 lines, imperative titles) hold across 53 files.

## What drags

1. **Four seas, one template (still).** Letter, bring a ring good, look at a place, bring a ring good, supplies, carry the settler. By goal kind, about 25 of the 33 arc and outpost steps are fetch-and-carry, and only 7 are "sail somewhere and look". Reading the four sea arcs back to back feels like one arc played four times.
2. **The south is the plainest sea** although it is the first door: tea, a garden to look at, drying racks. No set piece, no turn. The three later seas each have one.
3. **No question drives the story.** The winter "broke things", but nobody asks why or what is out there, and no step reveals anything. The captain never learns anything the player didn't already see. Curiosity is the missing engine, not conflict.
4. **The captain is a postman at the reunions.** Letters travel between siblings, but we never see Kit read Nell's answer or Ines hear from Oskar. The emotional payoff of every letter happens off screen.
5. **Repeated lines.** "Rook will want that in the book" closes all four outpost done cards; "Entered in the book, in good order" appears in both epilogue cards. A running joke once, a tic four times.
6. **Nothing happens after Four Winds.** The company has a name and a signpost, then only races and repeat asks. Skerry stops growing.
7. **Races are a second, separate story.** Wren, Kestrel and 16 races are charming but never touch the main arc, and Wren appears in the main chain only through one hint card.

## Gaps and bugs found

- **Ness's chart is invisible (#362, confirmed).** `watchman-passage` needs Ada's arc done and a call at Watchman Key. Ada's done card never mentions Ness, so a player who finishes any arc but Stonehaven first, or Stonehaven and then sails elsewhere, gets no letters and no hint why. A pure data fix.
- **Sea hazards are never tasks (#359, confirmed).** The only trace is optional text (Elias: "cross it in a lull", Sigrun: "keep off the ice"). No step needs a lull, a doldrum, an ice lead or an ash wind.
- **Far goods in asks (#360): partly done, premise needs correcting.** The keepers' own standing orders already ask for their raw good (Juno spice, Bram obsidian, Sigrun cloudberries, Elias amber). What is still missing: **no story quest asks for a far good**, **no ask crosses seas** (Sigrun wants Juno's tea; Bram's mirror for Rimewick is only mentioned once), and **all four settlers ask for timber three times** (Tam, Oskar, Nell, Pip).
- **Wren's standing order is at Saltmere, she lives at Abbey Head (#363, confirmed).**
- **Nell and Kit never speak to each other** even though both arcs set it up.
