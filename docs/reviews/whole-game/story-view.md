# The story view after Phase 4 (head storyteller, 2026-10-25)

For the thread "Review the whole game and the roadmap". Read against main e0023fa: the 19 story quests in order (rooks-thanks to four-winds), their English text, and the storyteller brief.

## 1. Can pirates fit the voice?

**Not as the roadmap writes them.** "Pirate ships that hunt loaded merchants", guns on every ship and a warship line break the brief's first rule (no villains, no danger to people, kids can read every line). They would also make Kids mode impossible to bring back and pull a sailing game toward a fighting game. I recommend dropping the guns track and the warship line.

**What fits: rovers you bring home.** Keep a chase, but make it a sailing hazard, not a fight:
- **The rovers** are crews of hard-up young sailors who left the islands when the trade died in the winter. The story already says so: Juno's "my pickers gone to sea", Fogwatch's "few boats go out now", Rimewick's eleven winters. They are not villains; they are the people the seas lost.
- **The chase:** a rover boat shadows a loaded ship and, if it comes alongside, takes a share of the hold ("a toll"). Nobody is hurt, nothing is fired, the ship is never lost. You escape by sailing well: a better point of sail, shallows they can't follow, a lit harbour. That rewards the skill the audience likes.
- **The arc:** in each sea, a person we already know recognises a rover (a picker of Juno's, an apprentice of Bram's...). The captain brings them home, one crew per sea, and they become crew or gatherers at the outposts. Each sea's rovers stop chasing once their crew is home, so the hazard fades as the story progresses, like the winter in Phase 3.
- **Switch off:** with Pirates off nobody chases, and each crew is met in a cove or harbour instead, so every quest stays playable. Each difficulty scales how often they chase and how big the toll is.
- **Ships:** the answer to a rover is speed and draft, not guns. If Phase 5 wants a ship or upgrade, make it a sailing one (lighter rig, signal lamp to call a harbour cutter), not a warship.

## 2. Is the story finished? What is weakest?

The four seas close well: four-winds signs the page and the company's book is full. It doesn't need a bigger plot, but it does need life after the arcs. What is weakest today:
1. **Four seas, one template.** Every sea is letter, bring goods, look at a place, bring goods, bring founding goods, carry the relative. By the third sea the player knows every next step. The hard seas (ice, ash, fog, squalls) are scenery the steps sail past, not something a step is about.
2. **People go silent.** Once their arc is done, nobody except Rook ever speaks again. Fourteen people with good voices, and after 3 to 4 hours the Quay tabs are empty.
3. **No pull after four-winds.** The reward is a little gold, then the sandbox. The company has a name but never does anything as a company.

A next chapter should fix those three, not add a new map: the rovers arc (above) uses each sea's own hazard in its steps, brings back the cast we have, and gives the company crews. Small, cheap help on top: **standing orders**, short repeatable asks from people whose arc is done (Sigrun wants berries, Kit wants salt), all quest-engine data.

## 3. The rest of the roadmap, from the story side

- **Racing missions:** a good fit for sailing fans. Give them a voice: a friendly rival captain or a regatta at a ring port, with Kit or Oskar as the starter. They could ship with Phase 5 as its second half.
- **Sandbox:** no story concern, as long as it has no quests and saves nothing.
- **Kids mode:** the story is already kid-safe; the rover shape keeps it so. Guns would not.
- **Rival companies (Phase 6):** only as friendly rivals (races, bids for a port's order), never as enemies. Low priority.
- **Steam and seeded maps:** no story need. Seeded maps can't carry the hand-placed story and fit the sandbox only.

**In one line:** keep Phase 5 as "pirates" in name and switch, but make them rovers you outsail and bring home, with no guns or warships, and use the arc to bring the cast back to life.

The ring review checks that the story's places still read right on the grown map (East Reef gap, Greyback Ridge, Teeth Rock, Watchman Key), that the winter damage shows in each ring port before its arc and is mended after, and that the five ring people don't go silent after their arcs (standing orders cover it).

Rule 12 of the brief changes so the giver names the plot and the choice, the building names coming from the build-spot data through placeholders so text never drifts from data: "Ada's plot by the water is yours, captain: {spotA} or {spotB}. Choose at Skerry." On the next docking at Skerry with an unchosen plot, a short arrival card in the giver's voice opens the build view straight on that numbered spot.
