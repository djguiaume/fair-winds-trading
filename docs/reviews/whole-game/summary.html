The whole game, and where it sails next

A look at everything on main after the Phase 4 release, with the head storyteller and the art director, and a proposal for Phase 5. Nothing gets built until you pick.

The answer

Yes for the game, no for the next step. Community arcs, the home island and manual sailing (decision 0120) are what made Phases 3 and 4 good. Keep them. What should change is Phase 5: pirates that hunt merchants with guns and a warship line break the story's first rule (no villains, no danger to people, kids can read every line), and they are the most costly thing on the roadmap.

Your two must-haves, first in Phase 5

1. The home island view, fixed. Today an arc opens a plot and the only hint is a four-second note; moored at Skerry you get numbered circles over the island and no harbour screen. Proposed: the arc giver names the plot and the two buildings on the done card ("Ada's plot by the water is yours, captain: weather glass or boathouse. Choose at Skerry."), the next docking at Skerry opens the choice on that spot, Skerry gets a harbour screen with an Island tab (plots, warehouse, repairs), and the circles only show while a choice waits. Mockups first.

2. The central sea reviewed like the outer seas. Every ring island at each town level and at night against the art bible, density, landmarks, the story's places on the grown map, each port's winter damage before and after its arc. Then a catch-up pass, screenshots to you. The art director already sees the gap: Westholm and Saltmere are a few small houses on flat green, and Westholm is dark at night.

Neither depends on the pirate choice, so both can start as soon as you say go.

What works

Reading the wind is the game, and every sea adds its own sailing problem: doldrums, ash, bergs, fog, squalls. The four seas look like one world. Every piece of content is a file with an editor and a command-line tool, so new quests and islands are cheap. The voice is warm, plain and safe for kids in both languages.

What is weak

  1. The sea is empty. No other ship ever sails, only boats moored at piers. For sailing fans, other sails are half the charm, and pirates, races, rivals and steam all need ships that sail by the wind.
  2. One story template, four times, then silence. Letter, goods, look, goods, found, carry. The hazards are scenery, never a step. The 14 people go quiet after their arc, and nothing pulls after the last one.
  3. Sound is thin. One music theme and two effects (creak, foghorn). No sea, wind, sails, gulls or harbour.
  4. The harbour hides the town. The port screen is a dark table, so while trading you never see the town your deliveries grew.
  5. Asked for, never built: racing missions and visible damage (both 2026-10-19), ship cosmetics.
  6. The roadmap is hard to read: 270 lines of history mixed with plans; the picture is out of date.

Three ways for Phase 5

APirates as written

Hostile ships, a guns upgrade, a warship line, combat.

For: it is what the roadmap says. Against: breaks the voice and Kids mode, costs the most, pulls a sailing game toward fighting.

Recommended

BA living sea, then rovers

Other ships sailing by the same wind, the sounds of the sea, then rovers: cozy pirates you outsail and bring home, then regattas.

For: fixes the empty sea, the silent cast and the sound, and builds the ships every later plan needs. Against: no real fights.

CFinish and ship first

A v1.0 polish phase: sound, the town in the harbour screen, visible damage, then the store plan.

For: fastest road to a game people can buy. Against: no new play, and the sea stays empty.

Option B, in short

  1. Other sails. Fishing boats near their port, coasters carrying what a town makes to the towns that want it, now and then a ship from a far sea. They tack like you do. A few per quality level, measured on the tablet test. All data, in the sea editor and npm run sea.
  2. Sounds of the sea: waves, wind in the sails, luffing in irons, gulls, harbour bustle.
  3. Rovers. Young crews who left the islands in the hard winter. A rover boat shadows a loaded ship and, if it comes alongside, takes a share of the hold. Nobody is hurt, nothing is fired, no ship is lost. You escape by sailing well: a better point of sail, shallows, a lit harbour. In each sea someone you know recognises a crew, you bring them home, and that sea's chases stop. The Pirates switch and the difficulty scale stay.
  4. Regattas, the racing missions you asked for on day one, with a friendly rival captain.
  5. Also moved in: visible hull damage, flags as data, a port screen header with the port's signature and keeper.
  6. Small things alongside: standing orders from people whose arc is done, and a harbour screen that shows the town.
  7. Roadmap: no guns track or warship line; Phase 6 only friendly rivals; seeded maps and seasons out of scope; the roadmap rewritten short, its history kept in its own file.

What the storyteller and art director say

They agree on the ring pass, the port screen and sound. They differ on pirates, which is your call (question 2).

Head storytellerPirates as written break the voice and close the door on Kids mode. Drop guns and the warship line. Keep the chase, make it a sailing hazard: rovers you outsail and bring home. The arc also fixes what is weakest today: four seas on one template, people silent after their arc, nothing pulling after four-winds.
Art directorPirates fit the look: the reference ad is pirates in this exact style. Keep it cozy by how harm is drawn: muzzle puffs, splashes and wood chips, never fire; nothing sinks, a beaten pirate strikes its flag and flees. First of all: the ring pass and a port screen header with the port's signature and the keeper. Bring visible hull damage and sea sounds into Phase 5.
