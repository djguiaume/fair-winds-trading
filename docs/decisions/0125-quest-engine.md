# 0125. The quest engine: many saved quests from data

**Status:** Implemented for review (2026-10-23). Requested by Guillaume ("yes" to starting the quest engine); the first work of [0120](0120-communities-and-home-island.md). Builds out the quest format of [0113](0113-levels-tutorial-and-music-as-data.md) and covers the step kinds listed in the arcs proposal ([community arcs](../story/community-arcs.md), [0122](0122-community-arcs-story.md), "What the quest engine needs"). No quest content ships: the arcs' stories and people are Guillaume's to approve.

## Context

0120 makes the people's arcs the spine of the base game: about 4 arcs of about 3 steps, each ending in a visible change to that person's port and a choice between two buildings for the home island. 0113 made the tutorial the first quest file (`src/data/quests/first-voyage.json`), but its runner (`src/sim/tutorial.ts`) runs only that quest, keeps its progress on the device (`fwt.tutorial.v1.<map>`), and moves on events the interface reports (chart opened, Continue pressed) with a coach mark per surface.

The arcs proposal needs: many quests at once with one tracked; starts on a finished quest or a discovered port; deliveries; quest cargo that takes hold space and cannot be sold; markers (one point slowly, several in any order, gates in order); "without touching the reef" (a hit starts the step over); timed runs from a dock with a par per difficulty level and free retries; a pass at night when night is on, at any time when it is off; finding a port from a rough circle on the chart; docking; rewards of gold, port changes and a two-building choice. Progress belongs to the voyage: saved, resumed and carried by the save code ([0066](0066-move-voyage-between-devices.md), FW2 of [0118](0118-compact-save-code.md), under 1,000 characters).

## Decision

1. **One file format, two runners.** Every quest is a file in `src/data/quests/` with the 0113 shape: `id`, `name`, `start`, `text`, `reward`, `steps`. A quest whose `start` is `"offered"` is the tutorial and stays on its own runner, unchanged. Every other quest's `start` is a **condition**, and the quest engine (`src/sim/quests.ts`, `QuestBook`) runs it. Moving the tutorial onto the engine would change where its progress lives and risk its tested behaviour for nothing the player sees.
2. **Steps.** A step has an `id` and either a `goal` (a condition: done when it holds) or `choose` (two or more options, each with an `id`, its own `reward` and optionally `next`; the player picks one). Optional: `reward` (paid when the step is done), `next` (a **later** step or `done`; default the following step), `clean` (touching a reef or shore, seen as new hull damage, starts the step over at no other cost), `port`, `chart` (`{ port, radius }`, the rough circle the chart draws) and `compass` (a point whose direction the compass marks, as for a foghorn) for the interface. The quest may have `items` (quest cargo: item → hold `space` per piece; 0 is a passenger, like the harbourmaster picked up from a wreck), `giver` (a person of `characters.json`, later), `port` (where its card shows) and `offer` (the player says yes first: once `start` holds the quest is offered, "I'll bring it" starts it with `accept`, "Not now" leaves it offered with nothing saved). Steps only go forward, so every quest ends and its progress is one step number.
3. **Conditions are a small code library** (`src/sim/questLibrary.ts`), like ship parts: `{ "is": "<kind>", …params }`. `port` may be `"home"`.
   - Anywhere: `always`; `all` / `any` (`of`: a list); `not` (`of`); `quest-done` (`quest`; the tutorial counts once finished or skipped); `chose` (`quest`, `option`); `docked`, `discovered` (`port`); `town-level`, `standing` (`port`, `atLeast`); `orders-done`, `gold` (`atLeast`); `has-cargo` (`good`, `qty`); `owns-ship` (`ship`).
   - Only in a goal, because they keep numbers while the step runs (their slots, saved):
     - `delivered` (`port`, `good`, `qty`): handed over there since the step began, sold or for an order; buying the good back there counts off again. Seen from the hold, so the market and the orders need no hook.
     - `carried` (`item`, `qty`, `from`, `to`): quest cargo, loaded alongside `from` as far as the hold has room (more trips in a small ship), unloaded alongside `to`. It takes hold space (`cargoUsed`) and is not a good, so it cannot be sold.
     - `reach` (`points`, `within`, `order` `any` or `in-order`, `maxKn` + `holdSec`, `atNight`): markers passed (also between two checks), gates in order, or one point held slowly ("under 2 kn within 12 for 5 s"). `atNight` counts only at night, or at any time when night is switched off in Settings (hazards stay optional).
     - `timed` (`from`, `to`, `via`, `within`, `par`): the clock starts when the boat leaves `from`, passes `via` in order and stops alongside `to` (default `from`); `par` is seconds, or seconds per difficulty level. Over par the run is off; dock at `from` and go again, as often as you like.
   A new kind is code once, with its check; a new quest from existing kinds is a file.
4. **Rewards:** `gold`, `standing` (port → orders' worth), `discover` (ports put on the chart) and `levelUp` (ports up one town level at once, the "step up" of 0120). The building choice is a `choose` step whose options will pay a `building` once `buildings.json` exists; a port's new look or production will read "quest done / option chosen" in that work. Until then the check refuses a `building` reward.
5. **Checks.** `checkQuests` refuses unknown kinds, params, ports, goods, ships, quests, options and items, slot conditions outside a goal, a step with both or neither of `goal` and `choose`, a backward `next`, repeated ids and unreachable steps; a test runs it on every shipped file. A quest naming a port the map lacks does not run on that map (the test seas, editor maps).
6. **Progress is part of the voyage**: `trade.quests` holds per started quest its step (or done), the step's numbers and the options chosen, plus the tracked quest. A started quest is tracked when none is; when the tracked one ends, the next active one is. It is in the save JSON (`quests`, optional: older saves have none) and checked on load: a quest or step no longer in the files, or numbers that do not fit, drop that quest's progress, so it starts again when its condition holds. Quests are checked four times a second of play, only while playing (0097: nothing moves while closed or paused).
7. **Save code.** FW2 gains a quest section at the end, only when some quest has progress, so codes without quests are byte for byte what they were and the save version stays 1. With it, a fingerprint of the quest, step and option ids (and each step's number of slots) is mixed into the header's fingerprint, so the reader knows the section follows and refuses a code made with other quest files ("made by a different version of the game"). The section is, per quest, 0 (not started), 1 (done) or 2 + step, the step's numbers, the option per choice step, then the tracked quest. Measured on the 30-port sea with five quests under way (two done, a timed run, a delivery, cargo aboard): **686 characters, 14 more** than without quests.

## Consequences

- The tutorial plays exactly as before: its file, runner and storage are untouched, and `tests/plays-the-same.test.ts` passes unchanged. With no quest files besides it, nothing changes in play.
- `tests/quests.test.ts` runs the engine on test files (`tests/fixtures/quests/`): starts, deliveries, quest cargo over two trips, markers, gates, a slow hold, a clean step reset, a timed run over and under par, a night pass, rewards, choices, chaining, tracking, saves and codes.
- Guillaume wants the tutorial reworked as the prologue (start at sea, sails and wind, a foghorn and a compass mark, a slow and clean approach to a wreck, the harbourmaster as a passenger, then today's chart, dock, buy and sell, and the island). The engine already has those sailing steps; the rework moves the tutorial onto it, with events for the interface-only lessons (chart opened, Continue) and its own proof that the trading lessons still play the same, once the story is approved.
- Next: a quest card and journal in the interface (static mockups first: card, tracked quest at sea, run clock, chart circle), then the home island's build spots and buildings, then the first arc behind a flag once Guillaume approves the arcs' story.
- Every new condition or reward kind needs a line in the library and its check; the save code needs nothing for a new condition kind (slots are written as numbers), and its fingerprint changes when a step's slots do.
- Renaming a shipped quest, step or option id resets that quest in old saves.
- Markers are map coordinates in the quest file, so moving an island in the map editor means moving its markers; named marks in the map file can come with the 5x map if this gets in the way.
